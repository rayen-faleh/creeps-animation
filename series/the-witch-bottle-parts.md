## The rules every part follows

1. **Hook (0–3 s):** a real, repeatable fact about that part's test, over its most charged image.
2. **Recap (one line, right after the hook):** over the bearded jug or the previous part's proof object.
3. **The test**, with a ladder of small questions every 5–10 s.
4. **A twist inside the test** (each part has its own; a newcomer gets a whole story).
5. **Cliffhanger:** unfinished action, hard cut, whisper of 8 words or fewer, title card "THE WITCH BOTTLE · PART n".
6. Closeness steps inward: town → street → your hands → your hearth.
7. Craft rules: `.claude/skills/short-form-horror/SKILL.md` and the gothic-pov-style skill (hands only, warm firelight
   indoors, cold outdoors, one red word per beat, unique in the script).

## Part II · The Needle (draft)

Its own story: *is the test real?* Twist: **the needle is a trick.**

| ID | Line | Picture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L01 | In sixteen forty-five, a witch-finder could prove you were a witch. With a needle. | A gloved hand turns a long brass needle in rushlight. |
| L02 | Last winter, we boiled a bottle of pins. And our neighbour came knocking, her hand burnt. | Recap over the bearded jug in the embers; the rag-bound hand at the door. |
| L03 | By morning, the whole lane knew it was Goody Ashe. | The lane: doors shut as a woman's shape passes. |
| L04 | The Witch-finder rode in two days later. Black gloves. A brass needle as long as my hand. | Tall hat in profile against the fen sky; the gloved hand. |
| L05 | He said the Devil marks his own. A spot that doesn't bleed. And can't feel. | The needle's point in close-up. |
| L06 | They took her into the church. I watched through the crack in the door. | POV through a gap in the church door, candles inside. |
| L07 | He pricked her arm. She screamed. Blood. | Her arm in profile; a bead of blood (one red drop, no gore). |
| L08 | Her neck. She screamed. Blood. | Same, closer. |
| L09 | Then her shoulder. Nothing. No scream. No **blood.** | The needle goes in; nothing. Red word. |
| L10 | Witch, he said. And the church emptied. | Silhouettes filing out past me. |
| L11 | He left the needle on the pew. | The needle glinting on dark oak. |
| L12 | I picked it up. I pressed it to my thumb. | My small hand, the point on my thumb. |
| L13 | And the point slid back into the handle. | Mute-proof turn: the point retracts into the handle. |
| L14 | It was a trick. She'd proven nothing. | Hold on my hand and the needle. |
| L15 | Then a gloved hand closed over mine. | The black glove closes round my hand. |
| L16 | *Hold out your hand.* (whisper, Witch-finder) | Hard cut. |

- Fact to verify before recording: retractable or blunt pricking needles were used by some witch-finders and searchers
  (see research.md R5; Reginald Scot's *Discoverie of Witchcraft*, 1584, describes a bodkin trick). Word it as "a trick"
  without claiming every witch-finder did it.
- New art: church interior (candles, pews, a door crack), the Witch-finder (tall hat profile, black gloves, book), the
  brass needle (with a retracting point), Goody Ashe in profile (shawl, bound hand), the lane by day in fog.
- Red word: `blood.` (check L07/L08 use "Blood." too: make only L09's red, or change L07/L08 to "She bled.").

## Part III · The Swimming (draft)

Its own story: *will she sink or float?* Twist: **she floats because of me.**

| ID | Line | Picture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L01 | In sixteen forty-five, if they threw you in the pond and you floated, you were a witch. | Still black water at dawn; a rope going into it. |
| L02 | If you sank, you were innocent. And drowned. | The water closing. |
| L03 | The Witch-finder caught me with his trick needle. So he made me hold the rope. | Recap: the needle in my palm, the glove; then the rope in my hands. |
| L04 | Dawn. The millpond. The whole village on the bank. | Wide: silhouettes on the bank, mist, the mill. |
| L05 | They tied Goody Ashe's right thumb to her left toe. | Her bound hands in profile (no faces). |
| L06 | And they gave the rope to me. | The rope wound round my small hands. |
| L07 | If I let go, she'd drown. If she floated, she'd hang. | Close on my grip. |
| L08 | They threw her in. | A splash; the rope runs through my hands. |
| L09 | Bubbles. The crowd counted. One. Two. Three. | Bubbles rising; the rope taut. |
| L10 | I prayed. Please. Let her live. | My hands tremble. |
| L11 | And she **floated.** | Her shape rises to the surface. Red word. |
| L12 | The crowd roared. Witch. | Silhouettes surge. |
| L13 | My hands were hot. The rope was smoking. | Smoke curls off the rope. |
| L14 | I opened them. Blisters. In the shape of pins. | Mute-proof turn: blistered palms, pin-shaped. |
| L15 | Across the fen, smoke was rising from our roof. | Our cottage far off, a thread of smoke (Gran's second bottle). |
| L16 | The Witch-finder looked from the water. To my hands. | The tall hat turns in profile. |
| L17 | *It's the child.* (whisper, `bf_emma`, from the pond) | Hard cut. |

- Fact to verify: swimming was used in the 1645 East Anglian hunt and was soon restricted (Wikipedia: Matthew Hopkins).
- New art: the millpond at dawn with a mill and mist, crowd silhouettes on the bank, rope (taut, smoking), a woman's
  shape surfacing (no face), blistered palms on the child hand (a new Hand overlay), the cottage across the fen.
- Grade: cold for the first time on a whole part; the smoke on our roof is the only warm thing.
