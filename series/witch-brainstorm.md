# Witch series — brainstorm (4 × ~60 s, 9:16)

Goal: drop the viewer into a village long ago, in the years when everyone believed in witches, and slowly bring the
threat closer: **the town → the street → the house → you.** Same gothic POV kit (hands only, candle/rushlight
memory grade, bm_lewis narrator, burned-in serif captions, blackletter title).

## Setting (shared by all scenarios)

- **Where / when:** a fictional fen village, *Mardle Fen*, East Anglia, **1645**: the real height of the English witch
  panic (civil war, plague, a bad harvest, a witch-finder riding from village to village). No real people are named;
  "the Witch-finder" stays a title.
- **Living conditions to immerse in (one detail per shot, never a lecture):** a one-room cottage of wattle and daub;
  smoke from a hearth with no chimney, hanging in the rafters; a rushlight (a reed dipped in fat) instead of candles;
  three to a straw bed, the pig behind a wattle screen; water from the well, bread black with rye; the church bell
  that rules the day; a curfew at dusk; babies that die and are named again on the next child.
- **Real folklore viewers can name:** witch bottles (stoneware "bellarmine" jugs with a bearded face, filled with
  bent pins, nails, hair and urine, heated or buried under the hearth); daisy-wheel marks carved by doors and
  fireplaces to trap evil; shoes hidden in walls; familiars (a cat, a toad, a hare); the "devil's mark"; swimming a
  witch; "watching" an accused woman, kept awake until her imp comes.
- **Narration:** first person, present tense, a child's eyes ("It's sixteen forty-five. I'm nine.") for immersion.
  Option: second person ("It's sixteen forty-five. You're nine.") like the Would You Survive? format.
- **Handling:** the real trials killed innocent people, mostly women. The horror should come from both directions:
  something may truly be wrong, *and* the village is dangerous. No gore; the swimming and the searches are implied.

## Scenario A — "The Witch Bottle" (recommended)

A real counter-spell drives all four parts: heat a bottle of pins in the fire and the witch feels it in her own body,
and must come to your door to make it stop.

| Part | Closeness | Story | Cliffhanger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **I · The Bottle** | the town | Hook: *"In sixteen forty-five, if your baby fell sick, you didn't fetch a doctor. You boiled a bottle of pins."* The cottage, the smoke, the rushlight, baby sister coughing in the cradle; three babies in the lane have died this winter. Gran fills the bearded jug: bent pins, nails, a lock of the baby's hair. Into the embers. *"Whoever did this will feel every pin. And she'll come to the door to make it stop."* | Midnight. The jug starts to hiss. **A knock.** Whisper from outside: "*It burns.*" |
| **II · The Neighbour** | the street | It was Goody Ashe, the widow two doors down, her hand wrapped in rag. She held me when I was born. By morning the lane knows. The Witch-finder rides in with his book. They search her for the devil's mark. The village turns: the stocks, the church, the market falls quiet when she passes. | They find a mark. She looks straight at me: "*Ask the child.*" |
| **III · The Swimming** | the house | She's swum in the millpond: if she floats, she's a witch. She sinks. The village calls it proof of nothing and goes home relieved. That night the baby is worse. Gran heats a second bottle. No knock. Instead, scratching… from inside our walls. | I look down. **My palms are blistering**, in the shape of pins. |
| **IV · The Mark** | you | I hide my hands. The Witch-finder is searching every house. Gran wraps my hands in rag, the way Goody Ashe's were. Twist: Gran isn't surprised. She opens her own hands: the same old pin-scars. She made the bottle to find out **which one of us it was.** *"It runs in the family."* | A knock. The Witch-finder. Gran opens the door, and turns to me. Whisper: "*Show him your hands.*" |

- Why it works: one ritual viewers can repeat and argue about; each part is complete (question → answer → new
  cliffhanger); the threat steps inward exactly as asked; the turn in III reads on mute (blistering palms); IV
  inverts who the ritual protected (Gran used it to find the witch in her own family, and Goody Ashe died for it).
- Recurring proof object for recaps: the bearded jug.

## Scenario B — "The Marks"

Every house in Mardle Fen has daisy wheels carved by the door and the hearth. *"The marks keep her out."*

| Part | Closeness | Story | Cliffhanger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I · The Wheels | the town | Life in the village; Father carves a new wheel beside the cradle each time a child dies. Cattle are sickening across the fen. | At dawn, the wheel on the church door has been scratched out. |
| II · The Lane | the street | Night by night, the wheels on the lane's doors are scratched out, house by house, and each house sickens. The village sits up to watch. | Ours is next: the house opposite is scratched tonight. |
| III · The Door | the house | We sit up all night. Nobody comes to the door. In the morning our wheel is scratched out… **from the inside**. | Scratching behind the hearth wall, where the marks are thickest. |
| IV · The Wall | you | Father breaks open the wall: a hidden child's shoe, and dozens of wheels carved on the inside. Twist: the marks were never to keep a witch out. They were carved to keep one **in**: this house was built around her. | The shoe fits my foot. Whisper: "*Let me out.*" |

- Strong, original twist and a great visual (marks scratched out, house by house). Weaker on ordinary living conditions,
  and the "keep it in" turn rhymes with the vampire long-form's threshold ideas.

## Scenario C — "The Watching"

The Witch-finder's method: keep the accused awake for nights; if her familiar comes to feed, she's guilty.

| Part | Closeness | Story | Cliffhanger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I · The Barn | the town | An old woman is chained in the tithe barn. The men take shifts watching her. Tonight it's my turn, at nine years old, with a rushlight. *"If anything comes to her, anything at all, you shout."* | Something small comes under the door. |
| II · The Hare | the street | It's a hare. It sits by her and stares at me. She whispers my name; she's never been told it. The next morning, the hare's tracks lead up our lane. | They stop at our door. |
| III · The Second Night | the house | I'm sent again. She tells me she's not the witch: "*It's in your house. Count the people asleep in your bed.*" | I get home before dawn and count. Four. There are three of us. |
| IV · The Fourth | you | Who sleeps in our bed? Twist: the hare has been in our house all along: my mother goes out every night and comes back cold, her feet muddy. | Mother's cold hand on my mouth in the dark. Whisper: "*Don't shout.*" |

- Most atmospheric and the most immersive in one place, but the cruelty (sleep deprivation) is front and centre, and
  "count the people in your bed" is more ghost than witch.

## Recommendation

**Scenario A, "The Witch Bottle".** It uses a real, strange ritual with a famous object (the bearded jug), the
closeness ladder is built into the plot, and the final turn is readable with the sound off.

## Round 2: Scenario A rebuilt so every part stands alone

Problem: in round 1, Parts II and III only *move the plot* towards the reveal (a search, a trial). A viewer who
lands on them cold gets no hook, no test, no payoff of their own.

Fix: **each part is built around one real 17th-century witch test**, and each test has its own twist. The series
becomes *four ways they found a witch in 1645*: every part opens on a fact someone will want to repeat, runs its own
test, turns it, and ends on a cliffhanger. The overarching story (Gran, Goody Ashe, the child) runs underneath.

| Part | The test (hook fact) | Closeness | Its own twist | Cliffhanger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I · The Bottle | heat a jug of pins: the witch feels them and comes to your door | the town | someone knocks | "*It burns.*" |
| II · The Needle | a witch's mark doesn't bleed or hurt: the Witch-finder pricks for it | the street | **the needle is a trick** | he catches you holding it |
| III · The Swimming | throw her in the pond: float, witch; sink, innocent (and drowned) | the house | **she floats because of you** | your palms blister in front of the whole village |
| IV · The Prayer | a witch can't say the Lord's Prayer all the way through | you | **Gran has done this before** | open: what Gran does at the door |

### Part II · The Needle (rebuilt)

- **Hook (0–3 s):** *"In sixteen forty-five, a witch-finder could prove you were a witch with a needle."* Picture: a
  gloved hand turning a long brass bodkin in the rushlight.
- **Recap (one line, over the bearded jug):** *"Last week we boiled a bottle of pins. And our neighbour came knocking,
  her hand burnt."*
- **The rule:** the devil marks his witches with a spot that won't bleed and can't feel. Find it, and she hangs.
- **Ladder:** where's the mark? (they search Goody Ashe in the church; we watch through the door) → he pricks her
  arm, her neck: she screams, she bleeds → the shoulder: **no blood, no scream** → guilty → the crowd leaves, the
  needle left on the pew → I pick it up and press it to my own thumb → **the point slides back into the handle.**
- **Turn:** the test is a trick. Goody Ashe isn't proven anything. So who is the witch?
- **Cliffhanger:** a gloved hand closes over mine. The Witch-finder, smiling. Whisper: "*Hold out your hand.*"
- Send-worthy: trick needles with retracting points are a real piece of witch-trial history (to verify and word
  carefully in research.md before scripting).
- Stands alone: a newcomer gets a full mystery (is the test real?) and a reveal, even without Part I.

### Part III · The Swimming (rebuilt)

- **Hook:** *"In sixteen forty-five, if they threw you in the pond and you floated, you were a witch. If you sank, you
  were innocent. And drowned."*
- **Recap:** *"The Witch-finder caught me with his trick needle. So today, he's made me hold the rope."*
- **The ritual:** dawn at the millpond, the whole village on the bank; Goody Ashe's right thumb tied to her left toe; the
  rope in **my** hands. If I let go, she drowns. If she floats, she hangs.
- **Ladder:** she goes under → bubbles → the crowd counts → I pray: *please let her live* → **she floats** (the
  expectation subverted: viewers assume an innocent sinks) → the crowd roars "witch!" → my hands are hot. The rope is
  smoking.
- **Turn (mute-proof):** I open my hands. **The palms are blistering, in the shape of pins.** She floated because I
  wanted her to. *I'm* the witch.
- **Cliffhanger:** the Witch-finder looks from the water to my hands. From the pond, Goody Ashe whispers: "*It's the
  child.*"
- Plant for IV: a thread of smoke rising from our chimney-less roof across the fen: someone at home is heating a
  bottle right now.
- Stands alone: a complete trial with a reversal (floating condemns her) and a personal twist, all in one location.

### Part IV · The Prayer (light rework, ending open)

- **Hook:** *"In sixteen forty-five, a witch couldn't say the Lord's Prayer. Not all the way through."*
- **Recap:** *"Yesterday at the pond, my hands burnt in front of the whole village."*
- **Story:** home, Gran wraps my hands in rag, the way Goody Ashe's were. Back at the hearth: a second bearded jug,
  still hot. Gran put it there. *"The first bottle, I put her hair in. This one, I put in yours."* She opens her own
  hands: the same old pin-scars. *"It runs in the family."* Knock: the Witch-finder. "*Say the Lord's Prayer, child.*"
  I get as far as *deliver us from*…
- **Ending options (pick one later):** (a) Gran opens her scarred hands to him: "*It was me.*" (b) Gran turns to me:
  "*Show him your hands.*" (c) Cut on my mouth trying to say *evil*.

### Why the series now holds

- Every part opens on a different real fact (pins, needle, pond, prayer): four distinct hooks, four reasons to send it.
- Every part has a test, a twist inside the test, and a cliffhanger, so a cold viewer gets a whole story.
- The overarching mystery escalates in steps a newcomer can catch from one recap line.
- Closeness still steps inward: town → street (the church) → the pond and your own hands → your hearth.
