# Werewolf series: brainstorm v3, three versions of the one-armed uncle

Shared setup in every version: a gang of kids on one street. Danny's uncle Mick is obsessed with werewolves and has one arm. He says a wolf took it, and everyone thinks he's mad. The narrator is **Sam** (a placeholder name, one syllable so it's easy to repeat).

Each version adds a different real piece of folklore, and that folklore controls the reveal:

| Version | Folklore (real) | What Mick did that looked mad | What it really was |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · THREE TIMES** | Call a werewolf by its baptismal name three times and it turns back (European / French loup-garou tradition) | Walked the street at midnight on full moons, calling kids' names under their windows | Turning Sam back, every month |
| **2 · THE DOG KNOWS** | Dogs sense werewolves; a dog will guard against one or bow to one | Never went anywhere without his old dog, and made every kid let the dog sniff their hands | The dog was Sam's guard |
| **3 · SAME WOUND** | Wound a werewolf and the person wakes up with the same wound (repercussion; the Auvergne paw-and-ring tale) | Stared at the kids' wrists every time they came round | He was looking for the wound he gave the wolf |

## Version 1: THREE TIMES

**Hook:** *"Danny's uncle used to stand under our windows at midnight and call our names."*

| Beat | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Hook / legend | "He only had one arm. Said a wolf took it." / "Full moons, he'd walk the street at midnight. Calling our names." | Child POV from a bedroom window: a one-armed silhouette under the streetlight, looking up |
| Spoken clue | "Everyone else, he called once." *(beat)* "Mine, he always called three times." / "Mum said I was his favourite." | The silhouette lingers under *this* window. Child's hand on the glass |
| Time jump | "Mick died on Sunday. Danny sent me a voice note he'd left." | Dip to cold. Adult hand, phone glow |
| Misdirect | Mick's voice, crackly: *"If I go, someone has to say his name. Three times. Every full moon."* / Sam: "Whose name?" | Phone waveform. Viewers think it's Danny's, or Mick's own |
| Night / turn | "I woke up outside." | Eyelids open on wet tarmac. The end of the road, the streetlight where Mick used to stand |
| Proof | His hands on the road: long black nails, grey fur to the knuckles. / "Mick never lost his arm to a wolf." *(pause)* "He lost it the one night he got to my name **too late**." | Push-in on the hands; "too late" in red |
| Cliffhanger | From down the street, Danny's voice: *"Sam."* … *"Sam."* … the hands claw at the tarmac … hard cut before the third. | Whisper: *"Sam."* Title **THREE TIMES**, PART I |

**Why it hits:** the creepiest thing the kids saw (a man calling their names in the dark) turns out to be the kindest. The ending stops on the second call, so viewers wait for the third.

**Series arc:**
1. **Three Times.** Sam is the wolf; Danny takes over the calling.
2. **The Baptism.** The name has to be his *baptismal* name. Sam's mum admits Sam isn't the name on his certificate.
3. **Wrong Name.** Danny has been calling the wrong name for a month, and something else on the street has been answering.
4. **Once.** Sam remembers that Mick called Danny's name only once, every time, even though Danny got the same call as everyone else. Danny needs one call, not three, because he's only just starting to turn.
5. **The Last Call.** Only one person knows Sam's real name, and they're on the other side of the door.

## Version 2: THE DOG KNOWS

**Hook:** *"Danny's uncle had one arm, one dog, and one rule. Let the dog smell your hands."*

| Beat | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Hook / legend | "Mick said a wolf took his arm." / "So every kid who came round had to hold out their hands to Bones." | Kids' hands held out flat, an old dog's grey muzzle sniffing them |
| Rule | "'Dogs know,' he said. 'Watch who the dog won't go near.'" / "We thought he was mad." | Bones sniffs each hand in turn |
| Visual clue | Bones licks every hand, then **lies down at Sam's feet and won't move**. Kids laugh: "He loves you." | Close shot: dog's chin resting on the child's shoe |
| Time jump | "Mick died in March. Danny's mum couldn't keep the dog." / "So Bones came to me." | Cold present, adult hands, a lead, an old dog in a new flat. A relatable rescue-dog beat |
| Spoken clue | "He's slept on my bed every night since." | Dog curled on the duvet |
| Night / misdirect | "Tonight, he won't come in." / Bones lies across the doorway, hackles up, growling into the dark. Sam does a torch search: under the bed, the wardrobe, the window. Nothing. | Torch beam sweeps; the dog's eyes catch the light |
| Turn | The torch swings back. Bones isn't facing the room. **He's facing Sam.** | Whip turn |
| Proof | Sam's hand on the bedframe: nails sunk into the wood. / "Bones never loved me." *(pause)* "Mick left him there to **guard** me." | "guard" in red |
| Cliffhanger | Bones stops growling. Slowly, he lowers himself and rolls onto his back, the way he only ever did for Mick. Cut. | Whisper: *"Good boy."* Title **THE DOG KNOWS**, PART I |

**Why it hits:** pets are the most shareable kind of horror, and "my dog won't come in" is a common worry. The final image says two things at once: the dog has given up guarding, and the only other person it ever rolled over for was Mick. So Mick was one too.

**Series arc:**
1. **The Dog Knows.** Sam is the wolf; Bones submits.
2. **Mick's Dog.** Old photos show Bones rolling over for Mick every time. Mick was a wolf, and the dog kept him in line for thirty years.
3. **The Other Arm.** Danny's mum explains the arm: Bones took it, the night Mick turned near the kids. Mick let him.
4. **Bones Is Old.** The dog is dying. Sam has one month left with a guard.
5. **The Puppy.** Danny turns up with a puppy from Bones's litter, and it won't stop growling at *Danny*.

## Version 3: SAME WOUND

**Hook:** *"Danny's uncle said he put an axe in a werewolf. And it took his arm for it."*

| Beat | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Hook / legend | "He said if you hurt one, the person wakes up hurt. Same place." / "So he knew exactly who it was. He just wouldn't say." | Kids on a garden wall; Mick's one hand turning an axe head on a whetstone |
| Visual clue | "Every time we went round, he'd look at our wrists." / "We thought he was mad." | Mick's torch moving along a line of kids' wrists, and stopping on Sam's |
| Spoken clue | "That summer I came off my bike into barbed wire." / "Stitches all the way round my wrist." / "Mum said I was lucky to keep the hand." | Child's wrist in a bandage. Innocent, sad |
| Time jump | "Mick died on Sunday. Danny asked me to help clear his shed." | Cold present. Full moon through the shed window; torch on |
| Misdirect | The shed wall is covered in pinned photos of the street, the kids, *Sam*. Creepy-obsessed-uncle confirmed. | Torch search across the wall; the viewer thinks "stalker" |
| Turn | On the bench, Mick's notebook, dated that summer: a drawing of a wolf's foreleg with an axe line across the wrist. Under it: *"Same wound. Same place."* | Macro insert, page turned by Sam's hand |
| Proof | Sam turns his own wrist into the torch: the white scar ring, exactly where the axe line is. / "I never came off my bike." *(long pause)* "I came off **Mick**." | "Mick." in red |
| Cliffhanger | Behind him, the shed door creaks. Danny's hand lifts the axe off its hooks. Danny: *"He said if he died… I'd have to finish it."* Cut. | Whisper: *"Sorry."* Title **SAME WOUND**, PART I |

**Why it hits:** it keeps the clean logic of the folklore. The "barbed wire" childhood scar most people half-believe is a real scar on *his* hand. And it ends with a friend about to use the axe, which is a bigger betrayal than any monster.

**Series arc:**
1. **Same Wound.** Sam is the wolf; Danny has the axe.
2. **The Bite.** Danny doesn't swing. He rolls up his sleeve: a bite scar from the same night. Sam didn't just take Mick's arm.
3. **Blood for Blood.** Real French lore: drawing a loup-garou's blood frees it. Mick's notebook says he *wanted* to be cut, and the arm was the price of freeing himself.
4. **Who Bit Mick.** The notebook goes back further: who wounded Mick, and where's the scar?
5. **Same Place.** The scar ring on Sam's wrist opens on the full moon.

## Comparison

| | Hook strength | Reveal shock | Sound-off read | Series legs | New art needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| THREE TIMES | ★★★ name-calling at midnight is instantly creepy | ★★ | ★★ needs audio for the calls (captions carry it) | ★★★ the name mechanic gives many twists | street exterior, one-armed silhouette, phone |
| THE DOG KNOWS | ★★ | ★★★ dog facing *him*, then rolling over | ★★★ fully visual | ★★ | **a dog** (new character, the hardest art), bedroom set |
| SAME WOUND | ★★★ axe + missing arm | ★★★ scar matches the drawing | ★★★ fully visual | ★★★ | shed set, notebook prop, axe, photo wall |

**Recommendation:** *SAME WOUND* for the pilot: it has the strongest hook and the cleanest twist logic. *THE DOG KNOWS* is the most shareable, but a dog is the biggest new art job. One option is to combine them: Bones the dog lives in Mick's shed in *SAME WOUND* and growls at Sam in episode 1 as an extra clue.
