# Werewolf series: brainstorm v2, "the one-armed uncle"

Replaces the Gran hook from v1 (it matched a previous series). New setup:

> A gang of kids on one street. Danny's uncle is obsessed with werewolves and has one arm. He says a wolf took it. Everyone thinks he's mad.

The viewer's instinct is "the uncle is the werewolf." Every direction below uses that instinct as misdirection and then turns it around.

---

## Hook options (first 3 seconds)

1. **"We used to dare each other to ask Danny's uncle about his arm."** *(recommended: a dare plus a missing arm gives an instant curiosity gap)*
2. "Danny's uncle had one arm, and a story about where the other one went."
3. "Every street has a mad uncle. Ours said a werewolf took his arm."
4. "Danny's uncle only had one arm. He said he'd lost it on our street."

Hook 1 lets the next line answer the dare: *"He'd tell anyone who asked. 'A wolf took it.'"* Then we hear the kids laughing.

---

## Direction A (recommended): **THE WATCH**

**The turn:** Uncle Mick was never the monster. On full moons he stood at the end of the road all night, watching the houses, because one of the kids was a wolf. The narrator is the one who took his arm, the summer they were ten. "A wolf took it" was literally true.

**Folklore it leans on (all real):** silver against werewolves; wolfsbane (aconite) hung at doors; the full moon; a werewolf that doesn't remember its nights.

### Episode 1, "The Watch": draft script (~140 words)

| Beat | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook (warm memory) | "We used to dare each other to ask Danny's uncle about his arm." | Kids' hands shoving each other on a garden wall at dusk; across the road, a man's silhouette with one empty sleeve |
| 2 The legend | "He'd tell anyone who asked. 'A wolf took it. On this street.'" / "We thought he was mad." / "Silver nails in every windowsill. Wolfsbane over his door." | Close-up: the child narrator's hand runs over a silver nail head in his own windowsill. **Visual clue:** the silver is in *his* sill, not Mick's |
| 2 The ritual | "Full moons, he'd send us all home before dark." / "Then stand at the end of the road all night." / "Watching the houses." | POV from his childhood bedroom window: a torch beam at the end of the road, pointed straight at this window |
| 3 Time jump (cold present) | "We called him Wolfman Mick." *(beat)* "He died last week." / "I came home for the funeral. Slept in my old room." | Dip to cold. Same window, twenty years on; the silver nails are rusted |
| 3 Spoken clue | "First full moon in twenty years with nobody at the end of the road." | Empty street, streetlight, no torch |
| 4 Night / misdirect | "I woke at three. Window open." / "Mud on the sill." | Eyelids wake. Casement swinging. The viewer thinks something got in |
| 5 Turn | "Then Danny texted." | Phone glow in his hand: *"Mum finally told me how Mick lost his arm."* … *"The summer we were ten."* … *"The night **you** went missing."* |
| 6 Proof | "I don't remember going missing." / Torch onto his own hand: mud under long black nails, grey fur at the cuff. / "Mick wasn't watching for wolves getting in." *(long pause)* "He was watching for one getting **out**." | Push-in on the hand; zoom jolt on "out" (red word) |
| 7 Cliffhanger | Out of the window: a torch beam clicks on at the end of the road, pointed at his window. Hard cut. | Whisper: *"Mick?"* Title **THE WATCH**, PART I |

**Why it flows:** the hook asks "how did he lose the arm?" and the text answers it. The question set up at second 1 is resolved at second 45, and the answer recasts every earlier line: the silver was in *our* sill, the torch was on *my* window, and "a wolf took it" was true. The torch at the end makes viewers ask a new question: Mick is dead, so who's watching?

**Sound-off read:** the silver in his own sill, the torch aimed at his window, the texts on screen, the claws.

### Series arc (5 episodes)

1. **The Watch.** The narrator took Mick's arm. Someone new is holding the torch.
2. **The Notebooks.** Clearing Mick's house, he finds twenty years of full-moon logs: dates, times, which window. Every entry has the narrator's initials, until the last page, which has **two sets**. There's a second wolf on the street.
3. **Silver Nails.** He goes house to house checking the sills of the old gang. Every house has silver nails except one: Danny's. Misdirect: Danny is the second wolf.
4. **The Torch.** He catches the torch-bearer: Danny's mum, Mick's sister, has kept the watch since he died. She isn't watching the narrator. She's watching *Danny's* window, and she's armed.
5. **A Wolf Took It (finale).** Mick's arm was never lost. It's in the notebooks' last photo, buried under the wolfsbane at the end of the road. It isn't human: Mick was a wolf too, the first one, and he cut it off himself to stop changing. *"He didn't watch us because he hated what we were. He watched us because he knew."* Then the narrator looks down at his own arm.

Each reveal turns the first episode's "mad uncle" a little further: monster → protector → victim → one of them.

## Direction B: **INSIDE OUT**

**Folklore (real, and creepy):** a Pavia account from 1541. A man claimed to be a wolf whose fur grew on the *inside* of his skin. Officials cut off his limbs to check, and he died of it.

**Uncle's story:** "They grow their fur inside. I had to check." The kids think he means he cut the arm off himself, and laugh. Present: the narrator has an itch under the skin of his forearm that won't stop, every full moon.

**Ep1 cliffhanger:** a kitchen knife resting on his forearm, the moon in the window. *"Just to check."* Cut.

⚠️ **Risk:** this reads as self-harm imagery. Instagram may restrict it, and it breaks the style's no-gore rule in spirit. It works better as a story Mick tells inside Direction A (episode 2 or 5) than as a series premise.

## Direction C: **FIVE OF US**

**The uncle's ritual:** after every full moon, Mick lined the kids up on the wall and counted them out loud. *"Five."* There were four of them. They thought it was a joke.

**Present:** Danny sends an old photo from that summer: four kids on the wall, and **five shadows** on the pavement.

**Twist options:** the fifth was always the wolf, standing among them; or the narrator was the one the others don't remember.

**Ep1 proof:** the narrator counts the shadows with his finger on the photo, and his own hand casts no shadow.

The image is beautiful, but the reveal is more abstract. It would make a strong mid-series episode inside A ("Mick counted five").
