# Hook & retention playbook for serialized shorts

Researched October 2026 for THE WATCH, Part III. Most sources are practitioner and marketing blogs; numbers in them
conflict and are unverified, so treat these as heuristics to test against our own Insights, not platform rules.

## What the sources agree on
1. **The first 1.5–3 s decide it.** Large early drop-off is consistent across sources; Instagram reports early
   drop-off ("skip rate" for the first 3 s). Hook with picture and voice together; no setup, no greeting.
   Check the first 1.5 s on mute: it must read without audio.
2. **Start in the middle of the action.** Storytime and TV cold opens begin at the most charged moment and let the
   viewer stay to learn how we got here.
3. **Newcomers need the premise in one breath.** TV's "previously on" is a 15–30 s entryway for new viewers and a
   reminder for returning ones; in a reel it must shrink to one or two lines inside the story (a line that serves
   both: new viewers get context, returning viewers get a reminder).
4. **Each part stands alone.** A complete episode (own question, answer, new cliffhanger) beats "follow for part 2".
   Label parts on screen and in the caption; post close together; group them in a profile playlist or series.
5. **Ladder of open loops.** Curiosity comes from noticing a gap between what we know and want to know (Loewenstein's
   information-gap theory). Open a small question every 8–10 s and close it before the next; the payoff must match
   the setup or it reads as clickbait.
6. **Signals.** Watch time and completion, then sends per reach (DM shares: the strongest reported driver of reach to
   non-followers), then likes. Build in a "send this to someone" moment: a rule viewers can test or argue about, or a
   twist they want to show a friend.
7. **Loops.** An ending that flows back into the opening invites rewatches; a supporting tactic, not a substitute
   for the story.
