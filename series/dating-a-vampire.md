# "Have you ever thought about dating a vampire?" — reel brainstorm

A new style for the channel: **horror-comedy, second person**, a week with a vampire boyfriend told day by day.
Same gothic POV kit (your hands, his grey clawed hands, moonlight and candle, serif captions); a deadpan British
narrator makes the jokes land.

## Research

### Lore vs Hollywood (what the contrast jokes can rest on)
| Topic | Folklore / source | Hollywood | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Looks | Slavic and Balkan folklore: the vampire is a recently dead corpse, often described as swollen and ruddy, blood at the mouth (Paul Barber, *Vampires, Burial, and Death*, 1988; argues these were misread signs of decomposition) | cheekbones, velvet, eternal youth | the hook contrast |
| Counting | European folklore: vampires compulsively count (seeds, millet, grains), so seeds were scattered at graves and doors (Wikipedia: Vampire burial; Arithmomania; Perkowski cited in folklore motif indexes) | gone | Day four gag |
| Invitation | must be invited in (folklore; Stoker) | kept | Day one: polite, then permanent |
| Reflection | no reflection (Stoker's Count casts none in the shaving mirror) | kept | Day three; callback in the twist |
| Sunlight | Stoker's Dracula walks by day, only weakened; *Nosferatu* (1922) popularised death by sunlight (SlashFilm; Mental Floss) | sunlight kills (or sparkles) | Day six fact gag |
| Native soil | Stoker's Count travels with boxes of his own earth | coffins | Day five gag |
| Stoker's description | hair in the centre of his palms, long sharp nails, very pointed ears, rank breath (*Dracula*, ch. 2; verify wording before quoting) | | optional hook line |

Verify before recording: the "swollen and ruddy" description (Barber's text) and the Stoker details (novel text).

### Storytelling method (what makes horror-comedy work)
1. **Benign violation** (McGraw and Warren, 2010): we laugh when something is wrong *and* safe at the same time. Keep
   each day's threat distanced (absurd, specific, low stakes) so it's funny; remove the distance on the last day so
   it turns into horror.
2. **Expectation → reality**: every day opens on the Hollywood romance beat (setup) and lands on the lore (punchline).
3. **Escalation**: inconvenient → absurd → unsettling → horrifying. The countdown (Day 1–7) is a built-in loop ladder.
4. **Specificity and deadpan**: a precise detail ("he brought his own soil") is funnier than a general one; the narrator
   never laughs.
5. **Callback**: plant a gag early (no reflection) and make it the horror twist later (*your* reflection).
6. **Button and send**: end on a one-line sting plus a question people answer in the comments and send to a friend.
7. From our own playbook: hook in 3 s on a mute-proof image; one red word per beat; a day tag on screen; end card with a
   comment prompt (`SurviveCard` / `DayTag` from the Would You Survive? format).

## Draft script (~145 words, ~60 s + end card)

| # | Line | Picture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L01 | Have you ever thought about dating a vampire? | Split frame: left, a handsome caped silhouette in candlelight; right, a lumpy shrouded corpse in moonlight. |
| L02 | Hollywood says cheekbones. Velvet. Forever. | Push on the left: a sharp profile, a rose. |
| L03 | Folklore says a swollen corpse, red in the face, who died last Tuesday. | Whip to the right: bloated, ruddy, a smear at the mouth. He waves. |
| L04 | Let's say you date one anyway. | Your hand takes his grey, clawed hand. |
| L05 | Day one. He won't come in until you invite him. So polite. | DAY 1. Your front door, him on the step, waiting. |
| L06 | Now he can come in whenever he likes. Forever. | His shadow already inside, behind you. |
| L07 | Day two. Every photo of you together is just you. Smiling at nobody. | DAY 2. Your phone: selfies with an empty space and a floating rose. |
| L08 | Day three. Dinner. He doesn't eat. He watches you eat. | DAY 3. Candlelit table; his untouched plate; his eyes. |
| L09 | Day four. You drop a bag of rice. | DAY 4. Grains bouncing across the floor. |
| L10 | That's your evening gone. He has to count every grain. | His clawed fingers sorting rice in tens. Clock spins. |
| L11 | Day five. He stays over. He brought his own soil. | DAY 5. A crate of earth beside your bed. |
| L12 | Day six. A sunny walk? In the book, Dracula strolled about in daylight. | DAY 6. Sunlit window. |
| L13 | Then a film in nineteen twenty-two said sunlight kills them. He's been sulking ever since. | He sulks in the dark under a blanket. |
| L14 | Day seven. Your anniversary. He says you look tired. Pale. | DAY 7. Your hand: paler. Candle. |
| L15 | Two little marks on your neck you don't remember getting. | Your fingers find two punctures (implied, no gore). |
| L16 | You turn to the mirror to check. | The bathroom mirror. |
| L17 | And there's no one there. | Red word. The mirror shows the room; behind where you'd be, his silhouette, smiling. |
| L18 | *Happy one week.* (whisper) | Hard cut. |
| — | End card: **WOULD YOU DATE HIM?** · *A WEEK WITH A VAMPIRE* · "Comment: Hollywood or folklore?" | |

- Escalation curve: polite (1) → sad-funny (2) → creepy-funny (3) → absurd (4, 5) → nerdy fact (6) → horror (7).
- Callback: Day two's "just you" → Day seven's "no one there": the gag becomes the twist (you're turning).
- Send-worthy: the rice gag, the 1922 sunlight fact, "Hollywood or folklore?"
- Red words: one per beat at most, e.g. `forever.` (L06), `grain.` (L10), `there.` (L17).
