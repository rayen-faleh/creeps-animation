## The folklore (real)

- **The mare (mara)** of Scandinavian and Germanic folklore sits on sleepers' chests at night, which is where the word *nightmare* comes from. Today we call it sleep paralysis.
- **She slips in through keyholes and knotholes.** Plug the hole while she's inside, and she can't leave.
- **The legend this story is built on:** in Danish, Norwegian (Telemark) and German versions, a trapped mare becomes a woman and marries the man. Years later he shows her the hole and pulls out the plug, and she's gone through it for good.

Sources: [D. L. Ashliman, *Night-Mares*](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/nightmare.html),
[Legends of the North: The Mare](http://legendsofthenorth.blogspot.com/2019/02/the-mare.html),
[Monstropedia: Nightmare](https://monstropedia.org/index.php/Nightmare),
[Stories, Fables & Ghostly Tales: Nordic folklore creatures](https://www.storiesfablesghostlytales.com/nordic-folklore-creatures-mare-backahast-pesta-metsanpeitto/).

- **The loop ladder:**
  - Why the wool? (L01)
  - What's a mare? (L03–04)
  - Did it work? (L08)
  - What happens when it's pulled out? (L10)
  - Who's on my chest? (L13)
  - Where's my wife? (L15)
- **Clues planted before the turn:**
  - Spoken: "That same week, I met my wife."
  - Visual: the long dark hair in the wool, her hair on my hand, the hair on her pillow.
- **The share moment:** the legend is real, and nearly everyone has heard of sleep paralysis. The post caption asks: *"Have you ever woken up and couldn't move?"*
- **Next-part plant (optional):** the ring left on the bedside table. Does she come back as his wife in the morning?

## Visual safety rules for this one

- No bodies, no coffins, no graves, no blood, no injury, no death in the narration.
- The mare is never drawn as a creature or a face. She is weight (the duvet pressing), breathing (the wool), hair and cold light.
- No faces: Gran is a silhouette, and the wife is a ring, a hand and hair.
- Sleep paralysis is shown as POV and pressure only. Nothing shows suffocation or self-harm.
- The opening frame is the keyhole and wool, which is calm, mysterious and safe as a thumbnail.

## Craft, matched to THE COUNT

- One location, with a recurring anchor (the keyhole, as THE COUNT used its window). Grades:
  - candle amber for Gran;
  - moonlit blue for the present;
  - cold keyhole light for the end.
- Hands do the acting, in two mirrored wool-pushing shots and the pull-out with the hair.
- **Sound:** breathing through the keyhole, a door draught, the duvet rustling, the clock, the heartbeat, and Gran's music box (detuned in the present). The whisper is a woman's voice (`af_nicole`, processed). That breaks the "narrator's own voice" habit on purpose, because the twist is someone else.
