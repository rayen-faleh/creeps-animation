# "Would you survive?" — Vampire (30 s short, brainstorm)

A new format: not a horror story with a narrator recounting the past, but a **second-person survival test**.
The camera is the *viewer's* eyes, the hands on screen are *their* new vampire hands, and the narrator tells
them what happens to them. Same gothic POV kit, grade, captions, voice (Kokoro `bm_lewis`) and sound engine.

Why it should hold: the title is a question every viewer answers in their head (participation), the
"Day one / Day two / Day three" countdown is a built-in loop ladder, and the punchline is a real folklore
rule colliding with an ordinary modern problem, which is the kind of thing people argue about and send.

## Format rules (from the short-form-horror skill, adjusted for 30 s)

| Beat | Time | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0–3 s | Mute-proof image (clawed hands, empty mirror) + the question in one breath |
| Ladder | 3–17 s | Day one → Day two → Day three: each day a folklore rule meets modern life; first two are "solved" (false comfort) |
| Turn | 17–23 s | The rule nobody thinks about bites, readable on mute |
| Punchline | 23–26 s | One line that reframes it (red word) |
| Sting | 26–28 s | Unfinished action, hard cut, whisper ≤ 8 words |
| Title | 28–31 s | Blackletter "WOULD YOU SURVIVE?" |

Budget: about 60–65 spoken words (~26 s with pauses at bm_lewis 0.97), plus the 3 s title.
A small on-screen "DAY 1 / 2 / 3" tag (Cormorant, upper left) carries the countdown on mute.

## Three concepts

### A. "You rent." (recommended)
The invitation rule: a vampire can't enter a home uninvited. Day one, no reflection, so Face ID fails.
Day two, sunlight: blackout blinds, sorted. Day three, you pop out after dark, come back, and your feet
won't cross your own threshold. **Because you rent. It isn't your home.** Then the landlord texts:
inspection tomorrow, nine a.m. (sunrise problem + invitation problem in one message).
- Send-worthy: every renter; "does the invitation rule apply to renters?" is a genuine comment fight.
- Mute proof: open door, hands pressing on an invisible plane at the threshold; phone text.

### B. "The delivery."
Night one, starving. You order in at 3 a.m. The driver texts "outside". He's on the doorstep and
won't step in. Neither will you. Twist: he hasn't moved, isn't breathing on the doorbell camera,
and is **waiting to be invited too**. Whisper: "Can I come in?"
- Stronger horror, weaker "survival test" premise; ends as a story, not a verdict.

### C. "Survival score."
Pure quiz: five quick rules (mirror, sun, garlic, running water, invitation), a ✓/✗ tally on screen,
"comment your score". Highest comment bait, lowest story; risks feeling like a listicle and
the gothic style suits story better than charts.

**Recommendation: A**, borrowing C's on-screen day counter. It keeps a twist and a cliffhanger
and is the most relatable.

Clue plants: the key ring with the agency tag is visible in Day three's first shot (visual); "someone's
home" in L09 sounds like a general rule (spoken) until L10.

## Production notes

- Reuse: Hand `vamp` style, Mirror (empty reflection), Phone, WindowWall (dawn mode), MoorStreet,
  DoorWall + under-light, Candle. New: BathroomBasin, roller blind on WindowWall, a door "threshold
  plane" effect (hands flatten on glass-like nothing, faint ripple), letting-agent key tag.
- Grade: cold throughout; Day two's dawn is the only warm light, and it's the threat.
- Sound: heartbeat absent (you're dead — a nice silent detail), drone, a sizzle on the sun flinch, blind
  rattle, keys, three knocks cut on the first.
- Runtime is set after narration is measured; trim words, not speed.
