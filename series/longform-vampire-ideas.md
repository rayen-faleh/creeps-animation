# Long-form vampire story (10+ min): hook ideas

Same gothic POV style, slower pacing, Kokoro `bm_lewis` narrator. Each hook starts from an ordinary habit with a
real piece of vampire folklore underneath.

| # | Title | Everyday trigger | Real folklore | Story seed / unique angle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | NEVER THROW AWAY your leftover garlic | The half-bulb in the cupboard, the clove scraped into the bin | Garlic at doors and windows; a clove in the mouth of the dead (Balkans) | Gran kept every leftover clove in jars under the beds, one jar per room. You bin forty years of it; that night something knocks at each room in turn, starting with the one you emptied first |
| 2 | NEVER FIX your grandmother's dripping tap | The tap that's dripped for decades | Vampires cannot cross running water | Gran opened it a quarter-turn every night so the house always had running water in it. The plumber fixes it; the house goes quiet; footsteps in a room locked since 1974 |
| 3 | NEVER let your cat jump over someone sleeping | The cat leaping across the bed at night | An animal leaping over a corpse makes it a vampire (Slavic/Romanian); hence the all-night watch over the dead | A wake; the cat gets in at 3 a.m. and jumps the coffin. Told by the one in the coffin, who isn't dead, only paralysed, and feels the change begin |
| 4 | NEVER cut down the hawthorn hedge around your house | New owners grubbing out an old thorn hedge | Hawthorn stakes; hawthorn planted round graves and homes; unlucky to cut | A cheap cottage ringed with hawthorn. The hedge was never keeping anything out. The village knew; now every neighbour locks their doors |
| 5 | NEVER say "come in" to someone you can't see | Doorbell cameras, smart speakers, "it's open!" | A vampire must be invited in | The doorbell camera records a 2 a.m. visitor using the elderly neighbour's voice, then your own voice answering "come in" while you slept. The story is a hunt through saved clips |

Front-runners: #2 (strongest trigger, natural slow burn: the silence is the horror) and #3 (most original POV).

Open decisions: which hook (or a mix: garlic jars + dripping tap as Gran's two protections), 9:16 vs 16:9,
five-chapter structure with on-screen chapter titles, hands-only POV vs on-screen characters.
Practical: about 1,450–1,600 words for 10–11 min; build and review chapter by chapter (full render about 1.5–2 h).
