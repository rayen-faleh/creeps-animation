# Rabenfels, 1732 — You Are the Vampire

A proposed interactive Gothic horror series for Instagram Reels. This document develops the world, participation format and introductory script; it is not a rendered video. All residents and the town are fictional.

The audience shares control of Elias, a vampire passing as a town clerk. The most-liked comment chooses his next action. Its author joins the town as an original human resident. The action happens on screen; the town's response carries into later episodes.

## Why 1732

The setting draws on the early eighteenth-century European vampire panics. Reports concerning alleged vampires in Serbia in 1725 and 1732 circulated through Habsburg administrative networks and provoked wider debate. The year is a strong historical anchor for widespread fear of vampires, rather than a claim that real vampires existed or that this was a measurable peak in their population.

Rabenfels is an invented fortified town in the Carpathian foothills. Its visual reference is the preserved medieval fabric of Transylvanian towns such as Sighisoara: enclosed streets, tradespeople's houses, defensive towers and an old church above the rooftops. Its medieval buildings still stand in an early modern world. Names and invented local customs belong to the fiction; this is historical fantasy, not a reconstruction of one documented town.

Working pronunciation: RAH-ben-fels. Series title: YOU ARE THE VAMPIRE. Location lockup: RABENFELS • 1732. Recurring line: The town remembers.

## Elias and the ordinary life worth losing

The audience is Elias Voss. He looks about thirty-five and arrived the previous winter, so his neighbours have not yet noticed that he does not age. He rents a shuttered room above the Broken Bell tavern and copies council records and private letters at night. Rent paid promptly and useful work make him welcome.

He is controlled, observant and dryly funny. He likes Marta's company and owes Matthias a favour. He has reasons to protect these people beyond avoiding discovery. He is also capable of exploiting them. The audience can move him toward protector, predator, manipulator or a mixture, through actions with visible consequences.

His routine: wake at sunset; work by candlelight; visit the tavern and listen; buy animal blood from the butcher in a covered cup; return before dawn. He sits with his back toward the tavern mirror so people looking toward him do not also see the missing reflection. Animal blood keeps him alive but leaves him weaker than feeding on humans would. That trade-off is an invented series rule.

The initial crisis: someone has killed a man and left him drained in the market square. Elias did not feed on this man. The frightened townspeople ask their trusted clerk to help investigate. The hunt threatens his cover even if he solves the murder.

## The recurring human cast

| Resident | What they control | What they want | How a viewer's action can change the relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Matthias, blacksmith | Tools, blades, locks and favours among craftspeople | Keep his street safe; repay Elias for helping with a council dispute | He can make equipment, hide Elias or recognise a suspicious commission. He is a friend before he becomes a possible hunter. |
| Marta, owner of the Broken Bell | Rooms, gossip, introductions and invitations | Keep her patrons and livelihood safe | She can shelter a witness or close her door. An accusation or a broken promise affects the tavern's welcome. |
| Otto, butcher | Elias's discreet animal-blood supply | Reliable payment and no trouble with the watch | His absence creates hunger pressure. His knowledge could become a bargaining tool. |
| Captain Grete, town watch | Gates, patrols and permission to investigate | Find a culprit before the crowd takes over | She can offer access or impose restrictions; she follows evidence and political pressure. |
| Father Anselm | Burial records, churchyard access and religious authority | Protect the living while preventing desecration of graves | He can challenge the town's assumptions and notice false records. He is not automatically a foolish fanatic. |

Only Marta and Matthias receive full introductions in the pilot. The butcher is introduced through Elias's routine. The watch and church gain names when the story needs them. Each scene should give the audience one face, one recognisable action and one reason to care.

## A town viewers can learn

The church and graveyard occupy the upper hill. The market square and council registry sit below them. Matthias's forge opens onto the northern side of the square. The Broken Bell stands on the western lane; the butcher works two doors farther downhill. The eastern gate leads out through the town wall.

These places connect consistently. A character leaving the forge toward the graveyard must take the uphill lane. A lantern glimpsed there can become evidence. A locked gate changes the available routes. Establish geography through walking, sightlines and recurring landmarks, without a long narrated map introduction.

Exterior palette: moon-blue slate roofs, narrow plaster and timber facades, dark stone walls, shuttered upper windows. Warm forge and tavern interiors contrast with the street. No gas lamps, Victorian police uniforms, modern bar fittings or later Victorian grave cages. Use candle and oil light, period tools and restrained clothing.

Preserve the existing kit's first-person narrator, bm_lewis voice, serif captions, grade, grain and sound language. Show human residents in readable profile or three-quarter poses; the narrator's own face stays unseen. Extend the reusable kit with streets and human acting rather than placing every conversation behind a door.

## Stable supernatural rules

- A private home requires its resident's invitation. Permission persists unless a later, clearly established story event changes it. Public tavern access follows its ordinary open invitation.
- Elias has no mirror reflection. Reflections must omit his hands and any part of him, while independently visible objects behave consistently.
- Direct daylight is dangerous and forces a return to shelter. This is the series' chosen rule, not a claim about every vampire tradition or the exact rule in Dracula.
- Animal blood sustains him with reduced strength. Human feeding offers power but can produce witnesses, harm and suspicion. Do not introduce effortless memory erasure to remove consequences.
- People retain their own will. A comment can make Elias propose, threaten, investigate or attack; it cannot dictate another person's consent or guarantee success.

Reveal rules through problems and action. Do not teach this entire list in the opening Reel. Invitation and missing reflection are literary precedents documented in Dracula; the diet/strength trade-off and exact daylight behaviour are original creative choices for this series.

## Comment control and casting

The on-screen request is concrete: What does Elias do before dawn? The leads invite immediate answers, while an original plan remains possible.

The most-liked comment at twenty-four hours after publication selects one actionable move within the established town and its rules. Ties go to the earlier comment. Show and pin the winning comment after the selection. From episode two onward, start the spoken narration with “You decided to <winning action>.” while that action is already happening. Credit the winning author briefly, then show an observable result within the opening few seconds. Do not imply that an Instagram default comment order is the voting record.

The winning author receives an original human resident connected to the action: a forge apprentice, tavern server, watch recruit, gravekeeper, courier, apothecary's assistant or merchant. Assign a period name and role, with the author credited. Do not require viewers to supply a full character biography to participate. A repeat winner advances their existing resident rather than producing a duplicate.

Examples:

| Winning move | New resident's role | Consequence to dramatise |
|---|---|---|
| Ask Matthias to make a false set of fangs | Forge apprentice | The order can protect Elias's cover or become evidence against him. |
| Question Marta's witness | Traveller lodging at the tavern | The witness has a motive to lie, flee or bargain. |
| Join the watch's patrol | Watch recruit | Elias gains access while being surrounded by people who could discover him. |
| Offer the butcher protection | Market trader | A useful alliance can shift suspicion onto someone else. |
| Inspect the burial register | Sexton's assistant | A discrepancy changes which grave is worth opening. |

Every winning action is performed or meaningfully attempted on screen. Reactions follow character motives and established facts. Avoid promising unlimited obedience and then quietly discarding an inconvenient winning suggestion. Explain the one-action/world-rule scope in the pinned format note.

Keep four to six residents active in a short run of episodes. Additional winners can enter through the register, a brief exchange or a recurring background task before a later story gives them a larger role. New residents persist; they are not automatically killed to make room.

Maintain a private continuity ledger: hunger, who suspects what, invitations, debts, injuries, clues, deaths, each winner's resident and the selection record. Show changes through behaviour: a seat no longer saved, a locked door, a hand trembling at a cup, a patrol waiting outside. A game HUD is optional and unnecessary for this visual style.

## Pilot: Night One — The Wrong Hunter

Target: about fifty-five to sixty-five seconds, to be locked only after actual narration measurement. Runtime is an estimate. The town/year label appears immediately, so the spoken hook can lead with the dilemma.

You're a vampire.
Your neighbours have asked you to hunt one.

Rabenfels. Seventeen thirty-two.

They know you as Elias, the clerk.
You write their letters after dark.
Pay your rent on time.

At the tavern, Marta saves your usual seat.
Its back faces the mirror.

Matthias, the blacksmith, calls you a friend.
He fixed your door before the winter.

You buy sheep's blood from the butcher.
Enough to keep your neighbours safe.

Until this morning.
A man was found drained in the square.
You never fed on him.

Now Matthias is sharpening the hunters' blades.
And Marta has hidden a witness upstairs.

The watch expects an answer before dawn.

What's your first move?
Top comment decides.
Its author joins the town.

### Caption for the Reel

Rabenfels, 1732. You're the vampire the town trusts.

What does Elias do before dawn? Give him one action. Question Marta's witness, follow Matthias, or choose another plan.

The most-liked move after 24 hours shapes Night Two. Its author enters Rabenfels as a human resident with a role connected to that move. Already in town? Your character's story continues.

The town remembers.

### Repeatable episode structure

From episode two onward, open with the spoken line “You decided to <winning action>.” Show the action already happening, credit the winning author briefly, and deliver its result immediately. This spoken acknowledgement is part of the repeatable format. Reveal one changed relationship or piece of evidence. Put one obstacle between Elias and his goal. End on a new specific action the audience can choose.

A viewer arriving at Night Four should understand the immediate problem without studying Nights One through Three. Recaps belong inside the consequence: You asked Elias to join the watch. Now they're searching his room.

Treat future episodes as responsive problems, not a fixed sequence whose outcome ignores the vote. Possible developing pressures include the butcher vanishing, a gate closing early, a witness recognising Elias's handwriting, the blacksmith requesting an invitation to Elias's room, or a new arrival claiming the dead man was seen alive after burial. Which event comes next must follow what viewers actually caused.

## Research and evidence limits

The participation format is a creative recommendation. It is designed to give comments lasting consequences and viewers a recognisable resident to share with friends. It is not an algorithm guarantee. Measure completion and sends alongside meaningful comments and return viewing. Meta publicly describes sharing as one among multiple ranking predictions; comments alone do not establish likely distribution.
