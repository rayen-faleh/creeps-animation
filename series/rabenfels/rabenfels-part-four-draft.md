## What the action costs

All three parts of the comment happen on screen, and each has a consequence:
1. **Jump back from the mirror:** Elias gets out of the glass before anyone looks. It works.
2. **Glare at Safwan:** it silences him, and it costs trust. He follows the watch upstairs and won't look at Elias.
3. **Tell them the perpetrator is upstairs:** the watch storms the stranger's room. But it's a *private room*, and
   its resident never invited Elias, so he cannot cross the threshold while Grete orders him in.

### Why it holds first-time viewers

- The opening plus the mirror image sets up the premise (he isn't in mirrors).
- L11–L12 teach the invitation rule visually.
- Otto's whisper is a mystery anyone can comment on.

The ending is deliberately different from Part III's "before he speaks": the secret has *already* passed to someone,
and that someone is the newest viewer's character.

## Alternatives for what's in the room

- **B · Empty room, earth on the floor.** A long crate of grave soil, Otto's bloody apron folded on the lid, the
  window open. Grete orders a chase across the rooftops; Elias can't follow on the street because the sky is
  greying. Cliffhanger: dawn versus pursuit. Less mystery, more action.
- **C · The stranger is still there.** He sits calmly on the bed, and in front of the whole watch he says: "Come in,
  Elias. You're always welcome." The invitation is a trap. Grete hears a vampire call the clerk by name. Elias can
  now cross, and the door is closing behind the watch. Cliffhanger: walk in, or reveal why he hesitated.
