# THE COUNT, revisited: why it went viral, and a new script in the same mould

## 1. What THE COUNT, Part I actually does (measured from the render and `shots.json`)

**Pace of pictures.** 18 shots in 64 s. Median shot about 2.7 s. The long ones (S10 5.5 s, S11 7.7 s, S13 7.2 s) are
continuous camera moves with a new thing arriving every 1.5–2 s: a tilt from the window down to the hands, the cloth
pulled off the mirror. The image never stands still for more than about 2 s, except reveal holds of about 1 s.

**Every shot has one action.** Pour, glint, dawn, sweep, eyes open, tilt, sort, freeze, pull, reach. Hands do the
acting. **There are no faces:** Gran is a candlelit silhouette and the narrator is only grey hands. The film never
shows the art we draw least well.

**Light tells you where you are in time.** Candle amber (the past), then dawn, then torch, then cold moonlight blue
(the present). Each grade change is a chapter break you can read with the sound off.

**One set, one recurring anchor.** The tracery window appears in about 7 shots: the hook, the dawn, the sweep, the
3 a.m. shot, the "still dark" shot, the opening casement. Viewers always know where they are.

**Story shape:**
- **0–3 s:** hook. Monster plus a childhood certainty.
- **3–13 s:** a little-known real rule, taught by an eerie, loved elder.
- **13–25 s:** the skeptic adult breaks it.
- **25–40 s:** a 3 a.m. sound, misdirected outward.
- **~42 s:** "the voice was mine."
- **42–64 s:** mirror proof, Gran's explanation, window, "One."

**Text on screen as a picture.** "Four hundred and six." The counting in italics works on mute.

## 3. Rules for the new script (taken from 1 and 2)

1. The hook is an image plus a real, little-known burial rule. "Smth NEW" is the draw.
2. An eerie elder teaches it in candlelight. A skeptic in the present doesn't follow it.
3. The self-twist hits around 50 s with one quotable line. **After it, at most 10 s, and no explaining.** The ending
   adds only a consequence and an echo.
4. The viewer is led to suspect the dead person, but the dead person is someone they never thought of.
5. **No faces:** silhouettes, hands and text on screen.
6. A new image or action every 2 s or less. Holds over 2 s only on reveals. Aim for about 24 shots in 62 s.
7. A grade-coded timeline: candle, then grey rain, then black with phone glow.
8. One recurring anchor: here, **Mum's texts** on a phone screen. They work on mute, can be read again on rewatch,
   and carry the visual clue.

## 4. The folklore (real, and new to most viewers)

The **Nachzehrer** ("shroud-eater") comes from German and Kashubian folklore:
- The newly dead wake hungry and chew their burial shroud in the grave.
- While they chew, their family sickens and dies one by one.
- The prevention was to put a stone, brick or coin in the corpse's mouth, or tie the jaw shut.

In 2009, Matteo Borrini found a 16th-century woman on Lazzaretto Nuovo, Venice, with a brick wedged in her mouth.
The find is the "Vampire of Venice", read as an anti-shroud-eater measure. Experts still argue about it, which is
good for the comments: some say the brick fell in, and others say it was placed when the grave was reopened.
Sources: [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/20619-vampire-plague-victim-spurs-debate.html),
[ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Technology/story?id=7022975),
[Archaeology](https://archive.archaeology.org/online/features/halloween/plague.html),
[ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/how-can-you-protect-the-living-from-vampires-these-are-the-tricks-people-really-used).

## 5. Script: THE SHROUD EATER (about 155 words, about 62 s plus title)

**The twist.** The narrator thinks he's lying awake in bed. He is in his own coffin. The "funeral last month" was
his. Mum didn't believe in the old ways, so there was no stone. He's been chewing, and the family is dying. Mum is
the last one left, and she's visiting tomorrow.

**Plant for a Part II:** Mum's visit. Does she bring a stone?

### Shot count and grades

There are about 24 shots, plus continuous moves in L12–L18 (the phone glow coming up, the thread pull, the torch
flood, the tilt down the shroud). The grades run candle amber (L01–05), then rain grey (L06–09), then black with phone
glow (L10–17), then torch white (L18–20), then a cold candle cut-in (L21). That is the same past/present colour logic
as THE COUNT.

- Coffin rim with lace pillow (candle).
- The wake through a door crack.
- Linen-thread macro.
- Mantel candle row.
- Top-down umbrellas and grave in rain.
- Lily on the lid.

- Quilted satin lid with buttons.
- Shredded shroud with grey hands (THE COUNT's `Floor` hands).

All of it is hands, objects and silhouettes. No faces.

- **Sound:**
  - candle and music box in the past;

### Safety

No gore. The chewing is sound plus threads. The dead are only hands, a coffin and an empty chair.

## 6. Alternatives, if the coffin twist feels too far from THE COUNT

- **Fifty knots.** Real folklore: a fishing net is laid in a coffin, and the dead must untie one knot a year before
  they can rise. Gran retied one knot at Grandad's grave every year for fifty years. She died in spring. Nobody has
  tied one since.
- **Face down.** Real folklore: prone burial, "so if he wakes, he digs the wrong way." Digging under your house,
  getting closer to the floorboards.
